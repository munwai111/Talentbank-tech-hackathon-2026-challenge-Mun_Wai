Full Advisor Responses

The Contrarian — "You're derailed and rationalizing."

You're not "on track with blockers." You're derailed and rationalizing. Here's the fatal flaw: PDF import is a sinkhole that's consumed 3 failed attempts with a 14-day hard deadline. Attempt #4 has no guaranteed success criteria. You don't know why it's failing. That's not debugging — that's gambling.

The CRITICAL bugs are worse than you're treating them. New users hitting 404s on GitHub/apply/skills routes means judges will hit those 404s during the demo. Hackathon judges don't give second chances.

The "AI-imported skills" showing wrong badges is a credibility catastrophe for a skills-first platform. The core value proposition is broken in a visible, embarrassing way.

Three priorities: 1) Kill the 404s today. 2) Fix the AI skills badge. 3) Drop PDF import until bugs 1-2 are resolved. Timebox attempt #4 to 4 hours. The uncomfortable question: what does the demo path look like right now, step by step? If a judge breaks it in under a minute, your feature list is irrelevant.

First Principles — "You're asking a sequencing question when you have a triage question."

What does a hackathon judge actually evaluate? They click through your demo. If new users hit 404s, the judge hits a 404 — that's a zero-score event. The PDF import is a nice-to-have. A broken core loop is a disqualifier.

Priority stack: 1) Fix the 404s immediately — one fix, three bugs resolved, do this today. 2) Drop PDF import entirely — you've burned 4 attempts, marginal demo value near zero. 3) Fix work history display — data saving to DB but never rendering is a broken feature. Judges will input data and see nothing.

What wins hackathons: a polished, complete core loop that works flawlessly beats a feature-rich demo that crashes.

The Expansionist — "You're one pivot away from winning."

Stop debugging PDF import — you're burning time on infrastructure while the judging rubric rewards experience and vision. The 3 CRITICAL bugs are actually your fastest path to an impressive demo. Fix them in 48 hours and you have a platform that works end-to-end for a new user.

Top 3: 1) Fix the 404s — 2-hour fix, do it tonight. 2) Fix work history display — most damaging demo failure. 3) Build one judge-facing "wow" feature: a skills match score between candidate and job as a percentage with breakdown. This is the thesis of your platform made tangible. Claude Haiku already integrated.

PDF import? Ship a graceful fallback — paste-your-resume text input. Frame it as "multi-modal import." Judges won't care; they'll care that AI analysis works on whatever input they give.

The Outsider — "You have 3 bugs that break the app for every new user. Fix those first."

A judge evaluating your demo will create a new account. They will hit a 404. They will stop evaluating your demo. The PDF import problem is irrelevant if the person testing your app can't get past the first screen.

PDF import has had 3 failed fixes. You are on attempt 4. That pattern tells you something: this problem is harder than you think. Meanwhile, every other broken thing listed is fixable.

Top 3: 1) Fix the 404s — one null-check away, do it today. 2) Make work history display — you already saved the data, rendering is a frontend task, do it tomorrow. 3) Add bio save feedback — a spinner or success toast, one hour of work.

PDF import: set a hard deadline. If attempt 4 is not working by day 3, cut it from your demo. Judges care that your concept works, not that one file format parses correctly.

The Executor ★ Voted Strongest 5/5 — "Stop debugging PDF import. Here's exactly what you do."

Stop debugging PDF import. You've burned 3 attempts. Ship attempt #4 only if it takes under 2 hours total — if you're not done by lunch Monday, kill it and move on.
